## Subscriptions for a new project

Subscriptions are collected in subscription periods, usually one per project and named after it ("Ewelme 2027"). To start collecting for the next project:

1. Open Financials and press Start new subscription period.
2. Check the name (it suggests the soonest active project's), the amount and the pay-by date.
3. Press Start. The button names the new period.

Everyone except exempt members then shows as Unpaid, and singers see the subscription card on their dashboard again. Payments for earlier periods are kept, under the ledger's Subscription period list. The name has to be new: reusing an old one would show that period's payers as already paid, so the page refuses it.

When a project is active but subscriptions are still for an older period, Financials says so at the top and links straight to the new period page. Saving a project as active offers the same link. Nothing changes until you press Start, because projects can overlap and you may not want to charge for every one.

## A new singer joins

1. Open Members.
2. Under Invite a Member, enter their email address. Their name and voice part are optional but save them a step.
3. Send the invitation.

They get an email with a link to set a password, valid for three days. Until they accept, they are listed under Pending Invitations, where Resend sends a fresh link. Once they sign up they automatically get an RSVP for every future event.

## A singer leaves

1. Open Members and click their name.
2. Untick Active and save.

They can no longer log in, and they drop out of RSVPs, the ledger and broadcasts. Their attendance history is kept. Tick Active again to bring them back. Accounts cannot be deleted from the site, so nothing is lost by mistake.

## Under-18 singers and their parents

An under-18 singer has their own record, under their own name and voice part, usually without a login. Their parent or guardian is linked to them as the person the choir contacts. On the members list a child shows "Parent: Jane Smith", and the parent shows "Parent of Alex Smith" in place of a voice part. Parents aren't counted as singers, so they stay out of the RSVP report and the ledger. An under-18 singer with no parent linked shows "No parent linked", which is worth fixing.

To add an under-18 singer:

1. Open Members and press Add an under-18 singer.
2. Enter their name and voice part.
3. Choose their parent if they're a member already. Otherwise give the parent's email address (and name, if you know it) to invite them.
4. Press Add Singer.

An invited parent is linked to the child when they sign up, and isn't made a singer. A second parent can be linked afterwards: open the singer's page, choose both under Parents or guardians (hold Ctrl, or Cmd on a Mac, to pick more than one), and save. "Invite a parent who isn't a member yet" there opens the invitation form set up for that singer. The Status filter's "Parents and guardians" lists every parent, and choosing a voice part also lists the parents of that part's under-18 singers.

If a singer turns 18, untick Under 18. Their parent stays linked until you remove them from Parents or guardians; the message after saving links straight there.

Some families signed up before this existed, with one account in the parent's name used for the child. To split one:

1. Open that member's page and follow "This is a parent signed up for their child".
2. Enter the child's name. The page says exactly what will move: the voice part, consents, medical notes, emergency contact, RSVPs, poll answers and subscription payments.
3. Press the button, which names both people.

The child then has their own record with all their history, and the parent keeps their login and email address, linked as the child's parent.

A linked parent signs in as themselves and sees a card for each child on their dashboard, where they reply to rehearsals for them. They also answer Looking Ahead polls for them, and keep their medical notes, consents and emergency contact up to date under Settings.

## Changing someone's role

Open Members, click their name, and change the ticks:

| Tick | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Singer | Gets RSVPs, appears in the ledger, polls, rosters and broadcasts. Untick for someone who has a login but does not sing. |
| Under 18 | Flagged on the Duty of Care roster and the members list, and asks for their parents or guardians (see above) |
| Exempt from subs | Shown as Exempt in the ledger and never asked to pay |
| Committee | Can use every committee page |
| Active | Can log in |

You cannot remove your own committee access or deactivate yourself, so the committee can never lock itself out by accident. Ask another committee member.

## Setting up a project

1. Open Projects and create the project, with its dates.
2. Set its status. Planning projects can have availability polls. Active projects appear on members' dashboards. Archived projects are history.
3. Choose its repertoire. To add a piece that is not in the library yet, use "Add a new piece to this project", which takes you to the Music Library with the project already chosen.
4. Optionally write a public summary. It appears on the Events page when one of the project's performances is next.

## Scheduling rehearsals

1. Open Events.
2. Fill in the form below the list: project, type, date and time, place.
3. Save. Every singer gets an RSVP for it straight away.

To add a rehearsal plan, click Edit on the event. The plan appears under the date on singers' dashboards, so it is a good place for "bars 1 to 24 of the Byrd, then the full piece".

Deleting an event also deletes its RSVPs, and asks first. Events in archived projects cannot be deleted.

## Putting a performance on the public Events page

1. Open Events and click Edit on the performance.
2. Under Public Listing, tick "Show on the public Events page".
3. Give it a public title and a short description. Add a ticket link and an end time if there are any.
4. Save.

The performance appears on the Events page as the next event until it has happened, then moves to Recent Performances and later to the archive. If the time is not fixed yet, set the start time to 00:00 and the page shows "time TBC". Rehearsals never appear publicly.

## Checking who is coming

Open RSVPs. Event Logistics shows each upcoming event's replies by status. You can change anyone's reply, for example when someone tells you in person. "Email Reminder" opens your own email program with everyone who has not replied in the BCC line.

Member Overview shows each singer's attendance over time, as the share of answered events they came to, lowest first. Singers who have not answered anything yet show "No replies yet" at the bottom of the list.

## Adding scores and practice tracks

1. Open Music Library. If the piece is new, add it with its composer and title.
2. Click the piece.
3. Leave the voice part as "From each file's name", or choose one (Full Choir for scores everyone uses).
4. Choose one or more files: PDF, MP3, M4A or WAV, up to 50 MB each. Each file becomes its own item, named after the file. Alternatively, paste a link to a recording elsewhere, such as YouTube, give it a name and choose its voice part.
5. Check the track titles (see below).
6. Upload.

The files appear straight away on that voice part's hub. Very large WAV files can time out while uploading; convert them to MP3 first. Deleting an item deletes the file too.

With "From each file's name", each file goes to the part its name ends with, which is how Dorico names part tracks: "Ave verum - Alto 2.mp3" becomes an Alto track for the Alto 2 line. That lets a whole set of tracks go up in one go. A file whose name doesn't end with a part (a full score, say) needs a voice part chosen. To correct a file afterwards, open Edit under it: you can change its name, voice part and part name, or take it out of the practice player, for example a recording that doesn't follow the same timing as the part tracks.

A track's title is the movement it covers, such as Magnificat or Kyrie, for a piece recorded as several files. Singers then see a tab for each movement above the player, rather than two buttons that both say "Bass". When you choose files, each gets a title box filled in from its name: "Gibbons - Short Mag - Bass.mp3" suggests Magnificat, and the common short forms (Mag, Nunc, Ky, Ag) become the full name. Change any that are wrong. Leave the box empty for a track of the whole piece, which is most of them. You can change a title later under the track's Edit. Tracks uploaded before titles existed have none: give them one with Edit where a piece has more than one movement.

## Marking sections for the practice player

Sections are passages singers can loop with one tap, such as "Fugue entry, bars 23 to 31". A section belongs to the piece, so it works on every part's track.

1. Open the piece in Music Library. If it has practice tracks, the practice player is at the top of the page.
2. Under Sections, give the section a label and, if you like, its first and last bars.
3. Set its start and end times. Either type them (1:04.2, or 64.2 seconds), or play a track, pause where the section starts, and press "Set from player" under Start, then do the same for End.
4. Give each part its starting note if you want singers to have "My note" for this section: E4, F#4 or Bb3, or two notes for a part that starts on a chord. Leave a part blank for no note.
5. Add Section.

Singers see a section once it has both times and "Show to members" is ticked, so untick it while a section is unfinished. Play loops a section in the player on the same page; Edit, the arrows and Delete change, reorder and remove it.

### Sections from the score

With the piece's score data uploaded, a section needs only its label and bars. Leave Start and End blank and save: the times come from the score, along with a one-bar lead-in and each part's starting note. To set it up, once per piece:

1. In Dorico, export the MIDI file and the MusicXML file, in the same sitting as the part tracks so they all agree.
2. On the piece's page, under Score Data, choose both files and press Save and Import.
3. Check the report: the number of bars, the length, and which parts' starting notes were found. Parts that aren't voices, such as an organ, are listed as ignored. If a part has landed under the wrong name, correct it under Parts; the name must match the part at the end of its track's file name, such as "Tenor 2". Tracks uploaded without a part name get one from their own name where it matches the score, and the report lists any track it couldn't match: give that track its part name with its Edit link and import again, or it gets no starting note.
4. Play a track. Under the report, the page says where the music starts and stops in the track and in the score data, and whether they line up. Silence after the music, which Dorico's audio export often adds, doesn't matter. If the track's music starts early or late throughout, it gives the audio offset to set; if the two drift apart over the piece, they come from different exports.

With score data, "My note" gives singers their next note from wherever they are in the track, so it helps after a long rest as well as at the start of a section. A piece imported before this was added needs Save and Import pressing once more to get it. The first import adds an "Opening" section covering the whole piece. A section marked "From score data" has imported times. Anything you type yourself is kept, even when the score data is imported again; clear both times to hand a section back to the score. If you change the piece in Dorico, export all three again and upload the MIDI and MusicXML here: the sections keep their bars and their times move with the music.

If every section plays slightly early or late, set the audio offset in milliseconds (a positive number moves them later) and import again.

### Sections for one track

A section can also belong to just one track, for a passage only that part needs, such as an alto descant. Under Scores & Tracks, press Sections on the track. The page then shows that track's own sections, loads the track in the player, and asks for a starting note for its part only. Add sections the same way as above; "Back to the sections for every track" returns to the piece's.

Singers playing that track see the piece's sections followed by the track's own. For a track with different timing from the others, such as a live recording, open its Edit and untick "Use the piece's sections", so it shows only its own.

## Asking about availability

1. Open Polling.
2. Choose a planning project, write the question, add the proposed date and any notes.
3. Create the poll. Members see it on Looking Ahead.

Results show the counts, each member's note, and how many have not answered, with a link that opens a reminder email to them. Close the poll once you have decided; it disappears from Looking Ahead. You can reopen it.

## Subscriptions

Open Financials. It shows the current subscription period; use the Subscription period list to look at earlier ones. To start a new one, see [Subscriptions for a new project](#subscriptions-for-a-new-project).

- When someone pays, check the amount and method and click Log. Their dashboard card disappears.
- Edit or Delete corrects a mistake.
- Mark Exempt for anyone who should not pay. Un-exempt reverses it.

Logging a payment while viewing an earlier period records it against that period.

## Emailing the choir

1. Open Broadcast and start a new broadcast.
2. Choose who it goes to: everyone, or one voice part.
3. Write the subject and message, and save the draft.
4. The preview shows exactly how the email will look and how many people will get it. Edit if needed.
5. Click "Send a test to me" and check the test in your own inbox. Tests are marked `[Test]` and go to nobody else.
6. Tick the confirmation box and click Send. Confirm when asked.

A broadcast can only be sent once, and cannot be edited afterwards. Each member gets their own copy, so nobody sees anyone else's address. An under-18 singer's copy goes to their parents or guardians instead, starting with a line saying whose parent they're receiving it as; a parent with two children in the choir gets one copy naming both. The preview says how many singers it reaches by how many emails, lists who each copy goes to under "Who it goes to", and warns about anyone who can't be contacted: an under-18 with no parent linked and no address of their own. The reminder links on RSVPs and Polling reach parents in the same way, and the RSVP report and Duty of Care roster show each under-18's parent with their phone and email. The Broadcasts list shows drafts and everything sent, with how many were delivered and whether any failed.

## The inbox

Enquiries and audition applications from the Contact page arrive here, newest first, with unhandled items at the top. The number on the Inbox link is how many are unhandled. Each submission is also emailed to the choir's address.

- Mark Handled once someone has replied. Mark Not Handled undoes it.
- To clear spam, tick the items (or Select all) and choose Delete as Spam. This asks first and cannot be undone.

## Duty of care

Duty of Care lists every singer's emergency contact, medical and accessibility notes, and under-18 flag. Singers fill these in themselves under Settings, and the roster says "None provided" for anyone who has not given an emergency contact. The roster is confidential: only the committee can open it. It works well on a phone, so it can be opened quickly at a rehearsal or concert.

## Documents

Documents is where minutes, policies and similar files go. The committee can upload and delete them, and every member can read them from the Document Vault.

## If something goes wrong

Every page that changes something shows a green banner when it worked and a red one when it did not, saying which field needs attention. If a page shows an error you cannot get past, take a screenshot and send it to me, with what you were trying to do. Django admin is still available to me as a fallback for anything the site cannot do.
